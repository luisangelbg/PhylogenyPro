AThe files the program reads and writes

PhylogenyPro is a desktop program that runs in the browser and sends nothing to any server: every file comes from and goes to a folder on your computer. This appendix lists them.

iA spreadsheet workbook is not read, and on purpose

There is no reader for .xlsx or .ods. Adding one would mean putting into the program someone else’s library of hundreds of thousands of lines, and it would stay inside forever. The choice was for the program to load not a single line that is not its own: that is exactly what lets it open with a double click, with nothing to install and no internet. Save the sheet as .csv and you are done.

A.3 · Keyboard shortcuts

Only a few: the program is mostly driven with the mouse. Alt + ←/→ goes to the previous or next block (in focused mode, section); Ctrl + K searches blocks, sections, controls and actions; [ collapses or shows the block bar; ? lists the shortcuts; Esc closes dialogs and panels, such as the interpretation help card and the image manager; and the space bar or Enter on a home-page card opens that block.

BThe catalog of models

B.1 · The 56 DNA models

Seven rate schemes, each with equal or empirical frequencies—fourteen base models—and each in the four variants of rate variation among sites: plain, +I, +G and +I+G. Fourteen times four, fifty-six.

Equal freq.Empirical freq.RatesWhat it adds to the previous one
JCF810The starting point: all changes equal.
K80HKY1Transitions are told apart from transversions.
TrNefTrN2The two transitions, with different rates.
K81K81uf2Two classes of transversion.
TIMefTIM3Different transitions and different transversions.
TVMefTVM4The four transversions free.
SYMGTR5All six rates free. There is nothing more general.

The +I adds a proportion of invariable sites; the +G, a discrete gamma with estimated α (Yang 1994), with the rates rescaled the way PAML does it. The right-hand column is, read from top to bottom, the chain of likelihood-ratio tests that the block can also run.

CThe decision rules, gathered

The scales the program applies to each number, in a single place. On screen you do not need to look them up: the interpretation help paints the band next to the value. They are here so you can cite them, argue about them and take them to a meeting.

!Half of these cutoffs are customs, not laws

The 70% for the bootstrap, the 0.95 for the posterior, the 200 for the ESS: they are reading conventions that the community adopted, not thresholds derived from a theory. The program says so in every card where that is the case, and this appendix repeats it once for all. Use them to orient yourself, not to decide on their own.

iThe three support scales are not the same

A 70% bootstrap, a 95% UFBoot and a posterior of 0.95 say roughly the same thing. Reading UFBoot with the bootstrap’s yardstick—celebrating a 75—is the most common mistake with the ultrafast bootstrap. They are in three separate rows for that reason.

DGlossary

Alignment (alineamiento)
The hypothesis that the columns of a sequence matrix are homologous positions. It is not a neutral preliminary step: it is the first result of the study.
Ancestral state (estado ancestral)
The reconstructed value of a character at an internal node. It is reported as probabilities, not as a winner.
ASDSF
Average standard deviation of split frequencies between independent runs. It measures whether two chains arrived at the same place.
Bayes factor (factor de Bayes)
The ratio of the marginal likelihoods of two models. It compares by integrating over all the values of the parameters, not with a shortcut like the AIC.
Block jackknife (jackknife por bloques)
Error estimated by removing contiguous blocks of sites, because neighboring sites are not independent.
Blomberg’s K
Compares the observed variance of a character with the one Brownian motion predicts on that tree. It measures magnitude, not shape.
Bootstrap (arranque)
Resampling columns with replacement. How often a branch reappears is its bootstrap support.
Branch length (longitud de rama)
The expected amount of change per site along that branch. In a chronogram, time.
Brownian motion (movimiento browniano)
A model of pure drift for a continuous character: the variance between two species grows with the time they have been apart.
Calibration justification (ensayo de calibración)
Putting in writing which fossil, which event or which rate each age comes from, with its prior. It is the most debated part of a dating study.
Chronogram (cronograma)
A tree whose axis is time, not change. It requires a clock and at least one calibration.
Clade (clado)
An ancestor and all its descendants. A group that leaves any of them out is paraphyletic.
Cladogenesis (cladogénesis)
What happens at the node when a lineage splits. In biogeography it is where the range is divided between the daughters.
Cladogram (cladograma)
A tree drawn without branch lengths: it asserts relationship and nothing more.
Concordance (gCF, sCF)
What proportion of the gene trees, or of the sites, contains a branch. It is a question different from support.
Consensus (consenso)
A summary tree of a set of trees. Strict, majority-rule, semi-strict or greedy; all of them throw away what contradicts itself.
Convergence (of a chain)
The sampler is already drawing samples from the posterior distribution and not from the path toward it. It is diagnosed with ESS, PSRF and ASDSF, never with just one.
Convergence (phylogenetic)
Two lineages that reach the same state by separate routes. It is the source of homoplasy.
DEC
The dispersal–extinction–cladogenesis model for geographic ranges (Ree and Smith 2008).
Discrete gamma (gamma discreta)
Rate variation among sites divided into a few categories, with a single shape parameter α (Yang 1994).
Early burst (estallido temprano)
A model in which the rate of evolution of a character decays with time; the model of adaptive radiation.
ESS
Effective sample size: how many independent samples your chain is worth.
Founder event (+J) (salto fundador)
A daughter that, at the split, leaves for a single area outside the ancestor’s range (Matzke 2013).
Homoplasy (homoplasia)
Similarity that does not come from a common ancestor: convergence, parallelism or reversal.
Independent contrasts (contrastes independientes)
Differences between sister nodes, scaled by their branches, that turn n non-independent species into n−1 observations that are independent (Felsenstein 1985).
Introgression (introgresión)
The passage of genetic material between lineages through hybridization. It leaves an asymmetry that incomplete lineage sorting does not leave.
Maximum likelihood (máxima verosimilitud)
The criterion that picks the tree and parameters that make the observed data most probable under the model.
MCMC
Markov chain Monte Carlo: the algorithm with which the Bayesian posterior is sampled.
Mk
A k-state model for discrete characters (Lewis 2001); Mkv adds the correction for having sampled only variable characters.
Molecular clock (reloj molecular)
The assumption that change accumulates at a comparable rate across lineages. Strict if it is a single rate; relaxed if it varies from branch to branch.
Monophyletic (monofilético)
Said of a group that is a complete clade.
Null range (rango nulo)
The state of a lineage that has lost its last area. It is absorbing, and it is what makes extinction real in the model.
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck
A model with a force that pulls the character toward an optimum. Its α is read as a half-life against the depth of the tree.
Outgroup (exogrupo)
A taxon known to be outside the study group, used to place the root. A bad choice shifts the whole history.
Pagel’s λ
Scales the covariances between species without touching the variances. It measures shape, not magnitude.
Paraphyletic (parafilético)
A group that excludes some of the descendants of its common ancestor.
Parsimony (parsimonia)
The criterion that prefers the tree that requires the fewest changes.
Partition (partición)
A subset of columns with its own model: a gene, a codon position, a genome.
PGLS
Generalized least-squares regression whose covariance matrix is that of the tree. It gives the same slope as the contrasts under Brownian motion.
Phylogenetic signal (señal filogenética)
How much more relatives resemble each other than expected by chance. A pattern, not a process.
Phylogram (filograma)
A tree with branch lengths: it also asserts how much change there was.
Polytomy (politomía)
A node with more than two daughters. It almost always means “I don’t know in what order,” not “they split at the same time.”
Posterior
The probability of a branch given the data, the model and the prior. It is not on the same scale as a bootstrap support.
Prior
What is asserted before seeing the data. In dating, the shape that declares the uncertainty of an age.
PSRF
Potential scale reduction factor (R̂): it compares the variation within each chain with the variation between chains.
Pybus and Harvey’s γ
Compares where the nodes of a tree fall with where they would fall under a constant rate. Negative, slowdown; but missing species mimic it exactly.
Quartet (cuarteto)
A group of four tips. It has three possible resolutions, and counting them is the basis of the species tree and of a distance between trees.
Robinson–Foulds distance
The number of splits present in one tree and absent from the other. It breaks easily: one misplaced tip takes it to its maximum.
Saturation (saturación)
The state in which so many changes have piled up that the observed difference no longer grows with time.
Split (división)
The division of the tips into two sets produced by an internal branch. An unrooted tree is its set of splits.
Split network (red de divisiones)
A figure that keeps the incompatible splits instead of throwing them away, drawing them as boxes.
Stochastic mapping (mapeo estocástico)
Drawing complete histories of the character on the tree, conditioned on the tips. It turns probabilities into counts of events.
Support (apoyo)
A measure of how confidently these data hold up a branch. It does not say that the branch is true, nor how many genes see it.
Tanglegram (tanglegrama)
Two trees facing each other, joined tip to tip. No crossings does not mean they are equal.
Ultrametric (ultramétrico)
A tree in which all the tips lie at the same distance from the root. A chronogram of living species is one.
Vicariance (vicarianza)
The division of a range in two by a barrier. In DEC one of the daughters keeps a single area; in DIVALIKE the cut can be of any size.

EWhen something goes wrong

What you seeWhat it usually is
“No row in the table” or “No areas in the table for” when crossing two filesAlmost always the underscore: Bursera_palmeri against Bursera palmeri. Block 9 forgives it; the others name it. Fix the file, not the list.
The tree comes out with a huge polytomyA consensus with many splits below the threshold. It is not an error: the data do not resolve that node.
The Bayesian chain does not convergeLook at the ESS and the PSRF before anything else. If the ESS is low in only one parameter, running longer is usually enough; if the PSRF is high, the chains are in different places and the problem is the model or the prior.
The branches come out extremely long in the Bayesian analysisThe branch-length prior. With divergent data, an exponential with mean 0.1 drags the whole tree down. The panel compares with the maximum-likelihood tree before running: heed it.
The OU wins but the half-life is enormousIndistinguishable from Brownian motion. Do not conclude stabilizing selection.
A +J model wins by a lotIt is to be expected. Report by how much it wins and over which model; do not turn it into long-distance dispersal without more.
The PNG comes out blank or the program refusesYou asked for more pixels than a browser can draw. Lower the dots per inch, or send the SVG, which has no resolution.
The pictures do not appear on the treeThe confirmation checkbox of each one has not been ticked. It is deliberate.
The colors of the exported SVG are not those on screenIt should not happen, and there are tests watching for it; if it does, export again from Block 12, which is where the figure studio hooks into the export.
The report cites something you did not runIt should not: the report is built from what actually ran. If you see it, it is a bug and worth reporting.
A block takes foreverCheck the number of taxa and sites against the table of sizes on the home page. Heavy computations run in separate threads, so the page does not freeze even if it seems not to be moving.
You lost everything on closingThe program does not save on its own. Build the Block 12 package before closing; it is a single button.

GThe tool: license and how to cite it

G.1 · What it is and what it is not

PhylogenyPro is a program written entirely from scratch: it loads no third-party library, no file from a content delivery network, no minified code. Everything it does—the likelihood engine, the sampler, the drawings, the ZIP writer—is in its own code and can be read.

What it does contain from outside are published numerical constants: the twelve amino-acid matrices, the aligner’s BLOSUM62 matrix, the genetic code and the geological time scale. They are not anyone’s creative expression: they are the parameters of published scientific models, and the file PROCEDENCIA.md documents where each one comes from and how it was verified.

G.3 · How to cite it

At the root of the program there are a CITATION.cff file and a codemeta.json file with the complete, machine-readable details; many reference managers read them directly. The short form, for the program and for this manual:

∑Suggested citation

Barrera-Guzmán, L.Á. (2026). PhylogenyPro: molecular phylogenetics in the browser, from the alignment to the dated tree (version 1.4.0). Universidad Autónoma Chapingo. Zenodo. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23005815

Barrera-Guzmán, L.Á. (2026). PhylogenyPro: User’s Manual (1st English ed.). Distributed with PhylogenyPro, version 1.4.0. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23005815

That is the concept DOI: it always leads to the most recent version, and it is the one to cite. To pin down the exact version you ran—the right thing when the result has to be repeatable—take its own DOI from the Zenodo record; for 1.4.0 it is 10.5281/zenodo.23005816.

In the methods section, also cite the works behind the methods you used: the substitution model, the type of support, the dating method, the biogeographic model. The Block 12 report gathers them on its own, and only the ones your study actually used, which is exactly the list that has to go there.
